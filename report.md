## **Part 1: Evaluation of Existing Mobile Applications**

### **1.1 Introduction**

The market for mobile language learning has moved beyond the nascent state, but there is a void that needs to be filled for an app that focuses on the phonetics of Mandarin Chinese, including the four tone lexicon, in a way that is appropriate for a child audience and tailored for the Hong Kong market. To compare ToneLens with the competitive offerings, three apps were chosen for comparison. Each app has a unique market segment within the overall range of language learning aided by AI, which ranges from visual-object-based vocabulary building (CapWords AI), Chinese text OCR and reading (Novli), and immersive learning (Memrise). The subsequent sections will discuss the ratings, pros, and cons of each app based on the available data.

### **1.2 App 1 — CapWords AI: Photo Vocabulary**

**URL:** https\://apps.apple.com/us/app/capwords-ai-photo-vocabulary/id6738896465

 

**Rating:** 4.6 / 5.0 (322 ratings on the App Store)

 

**Market Position:** CapWords AI is a visual vocabulary builder available in multiple languages and is used for general purposes. It has been awarded the 2025 Apple Design Award and App of the Year. The game involves turning real-world photos into flashcards with definitions and an example sentence and the original photo.

 

 

**Strengths:**

 

● **Visual anchoring pedagogy**: The research on dual coding theory and situated cognition indicates that the association of words with images has a greater impact on the episodic memory of the learner. CapWords AI has incorporated this by allowing the learner to use their own image as the background of the flashcard, resulting in improved retention by about 65%.

● **Contextual sentence generation**: The AI uses the background objects of the image to generate sentences that are relevant to the vocabulary.

● **Polished user experience and language support**: The app currently has support for more than 40 languages, and the user interface is aesthetically polished, which helped the app win the Apple Design Award.

 

**Weaknesses (Based on the app's reviews in the App Store, identify the following shortcomings):**

[**‎CapWords AI: Photo Vocabulary App - App Store**](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/capwords-ai-photo-vocabulary/id6738896465)

 

● **Lack of tonal instruction**. As a tone-neutral tool, CapWords AI treats Mandarin the same as Spanish or French. It does offer pinyin romanisation but does not provide the means for students to practice, hear, and be tested on the four Mandarin tones. This is a serious deficiency, as incorrect use of tone can completely change the word's meaning (e.g., mā 媽 vs. mǎ 馬).

● **Superficial assessment mode.** While the Quiz option does exist, it only allows for basic ‘Flashcards’ style ‘Recall from Recognition’. This does not provide for constructed response items, auditory discrimination, or production questions, making for shallow processing according to Craik & Lockhart's (1972) Level of Processing theory.

● **Lack of child-friendliness**. The interface does not appear to be designed for a child user. Font sizes and depth of navigation are not geared for children between the ages of 6 and 12.

### **1.3 App 2 — Novli (formerly Readly) — AI Scan and Read**

**URL:**  <https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/novli-learn-chinese/id6744056786>

[Novli - Learn Chinese - Google Play 應用程式](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.alexviola.bookscanner)

 

**Rating:** 4.8 / 5.0 on the App Store

 

**Market Position:** Novli targets intermediate or advanced learners who must read real Chinese texts, such as novels, restaurant menus, or handwritten notes, by laying pinyin and explanations on top of scanned images with the help of artificial intelligence.

 

 

**Strengths:**

 

● **Context-aware polyphone disambiguation**. Mandarin Chinese features many polyphonic characters (多音字) like 地 (dì/de), 為 (wéi/wèi), and 著 (zhe/zhuó). Novli's AI model correctly recognizes the applicable reading of each character, a function often beyond general OCR systems.

● **Integration of professional dictionaries**. Novli provides users with access to reliable dictionaries like Pleco, ensuring the academic accuracy of provided information.

● **Focus on authentic materials**. The app allows users to scan any Chinese text, which is helpful for extensive reading, a technique stressed by Nation (2001) as critical for learning beyond the 3,000 words vocabulary level.

 

**Weaknesses (Based on the app's reviews in the App Store and Play Store, identify the following shortcomings):**

[**‎Novli - Learn Chinese - Ratings & Reviews - App Store**](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/novli-learn-chinese/id6744056786?see-all=reviews\&platform=undefined)

[**Novli - Learn Chinese - Google Play 應用程式**](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.alexviola.bookscanner)

 

● **No object recognition capability**. As a text-based input app, Novli does not have an object recognition feature, which is available in other apps that use photographs. It is not possible for the app to recognize objects, and the pedagogical model is passive, as learners need to access written Chinese characters prior to receiving any help from the app.

● **No systematic quiz generation**. This has also been reported by users, who feel that without a built-in quiz, learners cannot engage with the app over a period of time. The app is useful for reading, but as Roediger and Butler (2011) point out, this is not a robust method for retaining learning, which is a critical aspect of language learning.

● **Not designed for beginners or children**. The app assumes that users are literate in Chinese characters, which is not possible for primary school students in Hong Kong, where they may need to learn Mandarin vocabulary for the first time.

&#x20;**1.6 Competitive Advantages of ToneLens**

Drawing on the gap analysis above, ToneLens establishes four principal competitive advantages:

 

1\. **Integrated tone pedagogy across multiple modalities**. Unlike the three competitors, ToneLens offers a complete tone pedagogy pipeline: AI-generated pinyin with tone marks → text-to-speech audio modeling → hand-drawn tone contour recognition → five different quiz modalities for tonal discrimination and production. This multimodal approach aligns with Mayer's (2009) Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning, which suggests that multimodal presentation can decrease cognitive load and increase schema development.

2\. **Context-adaptive quiz generation from real-world objects**. Unlike the three competitors, ToneLens offers a novel object-recognition-based approach to quiz generation. After a child takes a photo of, for example, 苹果 (apple), ToneLens's AI generates five different quiz questions, all related to tone, from a single object-recognition event. This type of retrieval practice, based on Bjork's (1994) desirable difficulties, ensures that a child's first encounter with a new word is immediately consolidated as a memory through effortful retrieval practice.

3\. **Child-centered Claymorphism design language**. ToneLens employs a design philosophy called Claymorphism, where the UI components are rounded and use soft shadow effects in a pastel color scheme. This design philosophy is child-centered and reduces anxiety for children, enhances the sense of playfulness for the user, and differentiates the app from the corporate design language of Memrise and Novli. The app's design is child-centered and specifically targets the motor skills and attention spans of children between 6 and 12 years of age.

4\. **Hong Kong bilingual localisation**. ToneLens is the only app among the four apps that offers a bilingual interface for the child, respects the L1 background of the Cantonese-speaking child, and offers example sentences using the words related to the daily life of the children in Hong Kong (e.g., 茶餐廳 items, MTR station names, local fruit market items). This is a crucial factor for the maintenance of motivation for the children, according to Gardner's (2010) socio-educational model of L2 motivation.

## **Part 2: Application Design**

### **2.1 Wireframes**

The set of wireframes was designed in Figma and covers some major screens of the application. Each wireframe demonstrates the structural layout, navigation hierarchy, and placement of interactive elements before visual styling is applied.

 

**Wireframe screens**

Key wireframe screens include:

● **Home Dashboard** - A central hub with three main action buttons (camera, history, settings) organized in a single-level navigation system for easy understanding for child users.

● **Photo snap Screen** - A two-point entry system for taking a photo or choosing from a gallery, accompanied by a large viewfinder area and a prominent shutter button.

● **Learning card Result Screen** - A screen showing the image of the identified object, traditional Chinese term, pinyin spelling with tone marks, tone number, and an AI-generated example sentence, accompanied by a "Start Quiz" call-to-action.

● **Quiz Screens (×5)** - Each of the five different quiz screens features a uniform interface consisting of a query area at the top, an interactive area in the middle, and a submit/next button at the bottom.

● **Tone quiz** - A grid overlay canvas featuring an undo button and a submit button, accompanied by a feedback overlay appearing after submission.

● **History Screen** - A grouped list of entries with a swipe-to-delete gesture, accompanied by star rating badges for each entry.

● **Settings Screen** - Language selection (EN/TC) and speech rate control for text-to-speech.

 


### **2.2 User Interface (UI)**

The high-fidelity UI mockups implement the Claymorphism design system with the following visual specifications:

 

● **Colour palette**: Warm pastels like soft coral (#F4A261), cream (#FEFAE0), sage green (#A8DADC), and lavender (#CDB4DB), with dark charcoal (#264653) for text.

● **Typography**: Rounded sans-serif fonts (e.g., Nunito / LXGW WenKai for Chinese characters) with large point sizes (at least 16 sp for body text and 24 sp for headings) for child-friendliness.

● **Component styling**: All cards and buttons have a borderRadius of 20 with an inner highlight shadow (at top-left with 40% opacity white) and an outer drop shadow (at bottom-right with 30% opacity matching the color) to mimic the 'clay' look.

● **Iconography**: Custom icons with rounded corners and thick strokes (3px) to match the soft look.

**Flowchart :**

---

**Node 0 — Startup**

\[INPUT] User launches the app on their device

\[OUTPUT] Animated splash screen displays the ToneLens logo with a bounce-in animation

\[OUTPUT] After 1.8 seconds, the app automatically navigates to the Home Screen

---

**Node 1 — Home Screen**

\[INPUT] User taps the **Snap & Learn** button

\[OUTPUT] App navigates to the Camera Screen

\[INPUT] User taps the **Smart Quiz** button (requires at least one saved learning record)

\[OUTPUT] App navigates to the Camera Screen to capture a new photo for quiz generation

\[INPUT] User taps the **History** button

\[OUTPUT] App navigates to the History Screen, displaying all previously learned words

\[INPUT] User taps the **Settings** icon in the top-right corner

\[OUTPUT] App navigates to the Settings Screen

---

**Node 2 — Photo Recognition Flow**

\[INPUT] User taps the **Take Photo** button

\[OUTPUT] Device camera is activated; live viewfinder is displayed

\[INPUT] User taps the shutter button to capture a photo

\[OUTPUT] Photo is saved temporarily and displayed as a preview

\[INPUT] User taps the **Choose from Gallery** button (alternative entry)

\[OUTPUT] Device photo gallery is opened; user selects an existing image

\[INPUT] User taps the **Recognize** button on the preview screen

\[OUTPUT] A loading animation is displayed while the AI analyses the photo

\[OUTPUT] On success: AI returns the Mandarin word, pinyin with tone marks, per-character tone data, Cantonese reference, and an example sentence; app navigates to the Result Screen

\[OUTPUT] On failure: A detailed error message is shown; user may retry recognition

\[INPUT] User taps the **Listen (🔊)** button on the Result Screen

\[OUTPUT] App reads the recognised word aloud in Mandarin (zh-CN) via text-to-speech

---

**Node 3 — Quiz Flow**

\[INPUT] User taps the **Start Quiz** button on the Result Screen or History Detail Screen

\[OUTPUT] App sends the word data to the AI; a loading animation is shown while 5 questions are generated

\[OUTPUT] Quiz Screen is displayed with a progress bar and question counter

\[INPUT] (Question type: **Draw the Tone**) User draws a tone contour shape on the canvas with their finger, then taps Submit

\[OUTPUT] AI evaluates the drawn shape against the target tone; correct or incorrect feedback with a written explanation is displayed

\[INPUT] (Question type: **Listen and Pick**) User taps the Play button to hear the audio, then selects one of 4 Chinese character options

\[OUTPUT] App highlights the selected answer in green (correct) or red with a shake animation (incorrect)

\[INPUT] (Question type: **Match Tone Shape**) User selects one of 4 tone contour diagrams that matches the target tone

\[OUTPUT] App highlights the correct answer and shows visual feedback

\[INPUT] (Question type: **Pick Pinyin / Pick Tone**) User selects one of 4 text options

\[OUTPUT] App highlights the correct answer with colour feedback and haptic response

\[OUTPUT] After all 5 questions are answered, the quiz attempt is saved and the app navigates to the Quiz Result Screen

---

**Node 4 — Tone Drawing Flow**

\[INPUT] User touches the drawing canvas with their finger (touch-down event)

\[OUTPUT] A new stroke begins; the starting point is recorded on the grid canvas

\[INPUT] User moves their finger across the canvas (touch-move event)

\[OUTPUT] The stroke path is drawn in real time; touch coordinates are continuously recorded

\[INPUT] User lifts their finger (touch-up event)

\[OUTPUT] The stroke is finalised; the Submit button becomes active

\[INPUT] User taps **Submit**

\[OUTPUT] The stroke coordinates are converted into a textual description (e.g., "rising curve from bottom-left to top-right") and sent to the AI for evaluation

\[OUTPUT] AI returns a judgment: whether the drawn shape matches the target tone, along with a bilingual feedback message (Chinese and English)

---

**Node 5 — Quiz Result Flow**

\[INPUT] User completes the final question of the quiz

\[OUTPUT] App calculates score and star rating: ≥80% correct → 3 stars; ≥60% → 2 stars; below 60% → 1 star

\[OUTPUT] Quiz Result Screen displays animated star rating, score percentage, accuracy progress bar, and a per-question breakdown showing the user's answer versus the correct answer

\[INPUT] User taps **Try Again**

\[OUTPUT] App navigates back to the Camera Screen to learn a new word

\[INPUT] User taps **Go Home**

\[OUTPUT] App navigates back to the Home Screen; learning statistics (word count, streak) are updated

---

**Node 6 — History Flow**

\[INPUT] User taps the **History** button on the Home Screen

\[OUTPUT] History Screen displays all saved words grouped by date, each row showing the word name and the star rating of the last quiz attempt

\[INPUT] User types text in the search bar

\[OUTPUT] The list is filtered in real time to show words matching the entered text (supports Chinese, English, and pinyin)

\[INPUT] User swipes a word entry to the left

\[OUTPUT] A delete confirmation dialog appears; upon confirmation, the record is permanently removed and the list updates

\[INPUT] User taps a word entry

\[OUTPUT] App navigates to the Word Detail Screen, showing the saved photo, word information, example sentence, and the history of up to the last 5 quiz attempts

\[INPUT] User taps the **Listen (🔊)** button on the detail screen

\[OUTPUT] App reads the word aloud in Mandarin via text-to-speech

\[INPUT] User taps **Start Quiz** on the detail screen

\[OUTPUT] App generates a new set of 5 questions for the same word and navigates to the Quiz Screen

\[INPUT] User taps the **Delete** icon on the detail screen

\[OUTPUT] A confirmation dialog appears; upon confirmation, the record is deleted and the app returns to the History Screen

---

**Node 7 — Settings Flow**

\[INPUT] User taps the **Language** toggle

\[OUTPUT] App interface switches between Traditional Chinese (繁體中文) and English; the preference is saved persistently

\[INPUT] User drags the **Voice Speed** slider (range: 0.3× to 1.0×)

\[OUTPUT] The text-to-speech playback speed updates in real time

\[INPUT] User taps the **Test** button next to the slider

\[OUTPUT] App plays a sample word aloud at the newly set speed for the user to preview

\[INPUT] User taps **Clear All History**

\[OUTPUT] A confirmation dialog appears; upon confirmation, all learning records are permanently deleted and the Home Screen statistics reset to zero

\[INPUT] User navigates back

\[OUTPUT] App returns to the Home Screen with all setting changes applied
